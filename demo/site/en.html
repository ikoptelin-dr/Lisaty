Propionibacteria: what is known about the species

Below are properties of the species and of individual strains in culture. They are not properties of the lysate: only measurement shows what survives in the product.

  • Propionic acid is the main fermentation end-product (Wood–Werkman cycle)[7]
  • P. freudenreichii produces short-chain fatty acids (propionic and acetic) and vitamin B12[8]
  • The species can synthesise the biologically active form of vitamin B12[8]
  • The microbiological assay read 20–40% higher than UHPLC, owing to analogues inactive in humans (pseudovitamin B12)[9]
  • The bifidogenic growth stimulator in the culture broth of strain ET-3 was identified as 1,4-dihydroxy-2-naphthoic acid (DHNA); about 10 mg/L[10]
  • Many properties of the species are highly strain-dependent[7]
  • Every species in the lysates, including Propionibacterium freudenreichii, is on EFSA’s QPS list (17.09.2026) — on condition that the strains carry no acquired resistance genes to clinically relevant antimicrobials[11]

Three layers of evidence — kept apart

Literature on species and lysates, data on this product, and permitted label wording are different things. We do not mix them.

Literature

About species and concepts. Not about our product.

  • ISAPP (2021): a postbiotic is a “preparation of inanimate microorganisms and/or their components that confers a health benefit on the host”[12]
  • Under ISAPP, such a preparation must contain inactivated microbial cells or cell components, with or without metabolites[12]
  • The ISAPP definition is contested: a group of authors published the response “Postbiotics — when simplification fails to clarify”[13]

Data on pharmaceutical lysates (medicinal products) are not transferred to a food ingredient.
